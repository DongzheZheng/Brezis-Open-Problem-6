Thus, with the radial-profile theorem, the published compact-perturbation minimality of the entire-space vortex, the standard zero-trace extension and strong-approximation bridge, and the sharp spherical spectral gap as four separately displayed inputs, Lean 4 verifies weak-energy minimality and almost-everywhere uniqueness in the equality case for every positive parameter when $n\ge3$. `#print axioms` for both the finite-ball and unit-ball public endpoints reports only `propext`, `Classical.choice`, and `Quot.sound`. This output audits the Lean proof terms; proofs and concrete instantiations of the four mathematical inputs are external dependencies.

`PhysicalRadialData` no longer separately requires a second-derivative witness, $C^1$ regularity of the derivative, a duplicate ODE condition, continuity of $\eta$ on the closed ball, or a left limit of the contact remainder at the outer endpoint. These facts are established by `PhysicalRadialRegularity`, `PhysicalProfileOrder`, and the top-level assembly. The remaining differences between the profile interface and the paper's cited, established radial-profile theorem are as follows:

- The paper gives positive smooth solutions $f_R$ on the natural interval $[0,R]$ and $F$ on $[0,\infty)$. Lean currently uses globally $C^2$ extensions defined on the entire real line. The extension of the finite-ball profile beyond $R$ and extensions of both profiles across the origin have not been constructed in Lean.
- At the origin, the paper uses the local smooth factorization $p(r)=rH(r^2)$. For the polynomial potential of this paper, its derivation from analytic elliptic regularity and $O(n)$ equivariance is summarized in the text. The printed statement of Ignat--Nguyen's Theorem 2.1 directly gives only $f(r)/r\in C^2$; therefore the stronger factorization must not be presented as a verbatim conclusion of that theorem. From a $C^2$ factor on the positive half-line, `OriginFactorTaylor` derives all **three fifth-order Peano expansions—for the function and its first and second derivatives**—in `RadialOriginTaylorInterior`. Together with formalized coefficient extraction from the radial ODE, it gives explicit formulas for the cubic and quintic coefficients. The public `PhysicalRadialDataCore` entry point invokes this theorem, so it no longer assumes Taylor coefficients or remainders independently. Still outside the formalization are the construction of the full-real-line $C^2$ representatives and factors from the published half-line profiles; local analytic regularity at the origin is part of the cited profile facts.
- `PhysicalProfileYExtension` now defines the auxiliary function $y$ in Lean to be $1-rF'(r)/F(r)$ at positive radii and zero at nonpositive radii. The origin factorization shows that its derivative at the origin is zero, so it is differentiable on the entire real line. The public `PhysicalRadialDataCore` interface no longer requires `y`, `hyDiff`, or `hyMatch`, and derives the internally used `hyFlt` from the natural published condition $F'>0$. Conversely, `profile_logSlope_lt_one` proves $1-rF'/F>0$; the two directions must not be conflated.

Existence, positivity, monotonicity, boundary values, the entire-space far-field limit, and the ODE itself are established profile facts cited by the paper. Formalizing them would involve a singular radial boundary-value problem and entire-space shooting or limiting analysis; the interface simplifications above do not replace those proofs. Until those constructions are formalized, the paper's formal-verification statement should explicitly identify the radial-profile theorem as an input from the literature.

The formal weak equality argument applies annular stability only to **smooth approximants** $u_j$. The coordinatewise bridge, quantitative Picone remainder, polar-coordinate summation, and energy bridge give $\|u_j-fe_r\|_{L^2}^2\le C_{\delta,\rho}(\mathcal E(u_j)-\mathcal E(fe_r))$ on each compact annulus. Strong fixed-trace $H^1\cap L^4$ approximation makes the right side tend to zero and gives strong $L^2$ convergence of $u_j$ to $u$ on the annulus; hence $u=fe_r$ almost everywhere. The paper now follows this equality-case proof.

The dependencies have two levels: the sharp spherical spectral gap and equivalence of Sobolev trace formulations are classical background; existence of the radial Ginzburg–Landau profiles and arbitrary-amplitude entire-space vortex minimality are established results specific to this problem. The mathematical proof cites their original sources, and the repository's main theorem includes them as explicit hypotheses. This distinction lets readers identify both the newly machine-checked deductions and the established analytic theorems on which they depend.
