# Formalization status and scope

The repository uses Lean 4.29.0 and mathlib v4.29.0. It writes the dimension as $n=m+3$, covering every $n\geq3$, and allows every parameter $\varepsilon>0$. Under four explicit analytic inputs, the public theorem [`physical_weak_unit_epsilon_minimum_and_ae_equality_from_core`](BrezisOP6/PhysicalWeakCanonicalYMain.lean) proves global minimality for the physical weak Ginzburg--Landau energy on the unit ball and shows that equality forces almost-everywhere agreement with the radial vortex. Companion theorems treat balls of arbitrary positive radius and identify the unit-ball base field through $f_\varepsilon(r)=f_{1/\varepsilon}(r/\varepsilon)$.

The formalized proof chain constructs the origin-regular logarithmic-slope defect from radial profiles, proves strict profile ordering and positivity of the first-contact remainder, establishes the Picone estimate for the zero spherical mode and estimates for the nonzero modes, and instantiates these estimates for the physical Euclidean energy. It proves a uniform quantitative stability estimate on every compact annulus for all smooth competitors, constructs strong $H^1\cap L^4$ approximations of weak fixed-trace fields defined by zero extension, passes the energy comparison and equality case to weak competitors, and verifies scaling of weak gradients, boundary conditions, and energy. These steps all lead to the public main theorem.

The four inputs are the established radial-profile theorem, the published minimality of the entire-space vortex, the classical zero-trace extension and approximation theorem, and the classical sharp spectral gap on the sphere. They appear as separate hypotheses in the Lean main theorem; their exact interfaces and sources are given in [`EXTERNAL_INPUTS.md`](EXTERNAL_INPUTS.md). The formal weak fixed-trace class uses a zero-extension definition. Its equivalence with the usual Sobolev trace formulation is part of the classical analytic background. The radial-profile and entire-space minimality results are established theorems specific to this problem. Proofs and concrete instantiations of the four inputs are outside this Lean development, as is the known two-dimensional theorem.

The full remote build, compilation of the root module, and the checks in [`AxiomAudit.lean`](AxiomAudit.lean) passed. The public endpoints report only Lean's logical axioms `propext`, `Classical.choice`, and `Quot.sound`; the source contains no `sorry`, `admit`, or custom `axiom` declaration. Reproduction steps are recorded in [`VERIFICATION.md`](VERIFICATION.md).
