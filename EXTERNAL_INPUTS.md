# Mathematical dependencies of the formal theorem

The public Lean theorems establish the $n\ge3$ finite-ball and all-$\varepsilon$ unit-ball energy comparisons, including almost-everywhere uniqueness in the equality case. Four analytic facts enter as explicit hypotheses. They are cited mathematical results or classical background, rather than custom Lean axioms. The comparison, contact, Picone, Euclidean energy, annular-stability, weak-closure, and scaling deductions from these inputs are proved in Lean.

| Input | Lean interface | Mathematical content |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Radial profiles | [`PhysicalRadialDataCore`](BrezisOP6/PhysicalProfileYExtension.lean) | The positive finite-ball and entire radial ODE profiles, boundary value, far-field limit, strict increase of the entire profile, and regular-origin factorization. The interface uses globally defined $C^2$ representatives and $C^2$ origin factors. |
| Entire-space minimum | [`PublishedC1VortexMinimality`](BrezisOP6/EnergyAdmissibility.lean) | De Giorgi minimality of the entire radial vortex against arbitrary-amplitude, compactly supported $C^1$ perturbations. The public theorem uses precisely this compact-test consequence. |
| Smooth fixed-trace bridge | [`StandardC1FixedTraceZeroExtensionBridge`](BrezisOP6/PublishedC1TraceBridge.lean) | Classical zero-trace extension and strong $H^1\cap L^4$ approximation for a smooth same-boundary field relative to the radial base. |
| Sharp sphere gap | [`SharpUnitSpherePoincareLocal`](BrezisOP6/SphereLocalPoincare.lean) | The sharp scalar Poincaré inequality on the round $S^{n-1}$, whose first positive eigenvalue is $n-1$. |

The radial-profile existence, uniqueness, and monotonicity used here are established in [Ignat–Nguyen–Slastikov–Zarnescu, *SIAM Journal on Mathematical Analysis* 46 (2014), Theorem 1.1](https://doi.org/10.1137/130948598). Their Proposition 2.2 supplies origin regularity for the quotient $p(r)/r$. The stronger local factorization $p(r)=rH(r^2)$ used in the Lean interface follows from analytic elliptic regularity for the polynomial potential and orthogonal equivariance; see, for the classical regularity ingredient, [Morrey (1958)](https://doi.org/10.2307/2372830). The passage from these half-line profiles to the interface's full-real-line $C^2$ representatives and factors is part of the cited profile input, rather than a theorem proved in Lean.

For the entire-space minimum, the three-dimensional source is [Millot–Pisante, *Journal of the European Mathematical Society* 12 (2010), Theorem 1.4](https://doi.org/10.4171/JEMS/223); higher dimensions use [Pisante, *Journal of Functional Analysis* 260 (2011)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jfa.2010.09.002). This is the nonradial, arbitrary-amplitude comparison required at the start of the energy argument. Its compact-test form is kept separate from the standard fixed-trace bridge. [`PublishedC1TraceBridge.lean`](BrezisOP6/PublishedC1TraceBridge.lean) proves that their conjunction yields the same-boundary finite-ball comparison, including the energy limit under strong $H^1\cap L^4$ convergence.

The sharp sphere gap and the smooth-ball trace/zero-extension theorem are classical spectral and Sobolev inputs. The latter identifies the usual fixed trace with the zero-extension condition used by [`WeakFixedTraceCompetitor`](BrezisOP6/WeakBallEnergy.lean). The formal competitor carries an actual distributional weak gradient; its difference from the radial base has zero extension in $H^1\cap L^4$. Within this certified class, [`WeakFixedTraceDensity.lean`](BrezisOP6/WeakFixedTraceDensity.lean) constructs fixed-trace smooth approximants, and [`WeakEnergyStrongConvergence.lean`](BrezisOP6/WeakEnergyStrongConvergence.lean) proves convergence of the physical energy. These are proof steps, not additional external closure hypotheses.

From the profile interface, [`OriginFactorTaylor.lean`](BrezisOP6/OriginFactorTaylor.lean) computes the differentiated fifth-order origin expansion, and [`PhysicalProfileYExtension.lean`](BrezisOP6/PhysicalProfileYExtension.lean) constructs a differentiable extension of $1-rF'(r)/F(r)$. The public theorem assumes neither Taylor coefficients nor an auxiliary slope-defect function. The quantitative annular estimate in [`ActualSmoothAnnularEnergyGap.lean`](BrezisOP6/ActualSmoothAnnularEnergyGap.lean) applies to smooth approximants and passes to equality cases by strong convergence; it does not require a separate regularity theorem for a weak equality case.

Thus the formalization verifies the new $n\ge3$ deduction in full **under the four displayed analytic inputs**. The proofs of the profile and entire-vortex theorems, the classical sphere gap, the smooth-ball trace identification, and the application of the cited profile regularity to the exact Lean representatives remain mathematical dependencies outside this repository. The established $n=2$ case is also outside its scope.
